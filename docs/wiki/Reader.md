# Reader

The Reader (`/reader`, in the main navigation) is part of the app; you can switch it off for yourself in [Settings → Features](Settings-tour#features). It is a library of books you import, with a lookup popup in the style of Yomitan, bookmarks, highlights and notes. The library belongs to a workspace: the library page lists the current workspace's books only, and reading time counts for the book's workspace. If the current library is empty but other workspaces hold books, the page lists them with a button to switch.

## Importing books

The Reader takes Chinese and Japanese books; in a workspace of another language the library says so.

- **Add an epub** (up to 20 MiB). Books go to the current workspace's library and must be in its language ("Books go to this workspace's library and must be in Chinese. A file that does not declare its language is read as Chinese."). A Chinese book cannot go into a Japanese workspace; the page asks you to switch. The same epub may live in several workspaces as separate copies.
- **Browse public-domain texts** (the link under Add an epub, `/reader/sources`): one tab per source, Aozora Bunko (public domain works only) and Project Gutenberg (public domain in the USA). The first source serving the workspace language is selected, so a Chinese workspace opens on Gutenberg and a Japanese one on Aozora.
  - **Suggestions for today**: 12 works, the same all day (they are chosen by your date); **Shuffle** draws another set. The first visit downloads the catalogue, which can take a moment.
  - **Search** ("Title, author, or romaji / pinyin") matches title and author as written, and also by reading: romaji for Aozora (`tokyo`, `Tōkyō` and `toukyou` find 東京) and pinyin for Gutenberg's Chinese works (`hong lou meng`, `hóng-lóu-mèng`). The matching reading is shown under the hit ("Matched reading: …"); written-form matches come first. A reading query needs at least 3 letters after long vowels are folded (`tou` is too short).
  - Romaji is forgiving: kunrei-shiki and Nihon-shiki spellings fold to Hepburn (`si`, `tu`, `syu`, `tyuu`, `hu` find the same titles as `shi`, `tsu`, `shu`, `chuu`, `fu`), `wo` folds to `o`, and a standalone `wa` or `e` is read as the particle は or へ, as in `konnichiwa`: `wagahai wa neko de aru` finds 吾輩は猫である. Particles are not recognised inside hyphenated pieces (`watashi-wa`) or inside a longer word (`wagahaiwa`).
  - Known limits: Gutenberg's Japanese titles are not searchable by reading, and neither are Gutenberg Chinese works whose pinyin could not be indexed yet (the index is rebuilt when the catalogue is refreshed, every 7 days).
  - **Show all languages** also lists results in other languages; such a result shows "Needs a <language> workspace" instead of **Import**. A book already in the library says **In library**.

Imports run on the server, one work per request, from an allow-list of hosts. Each imported book keeps its source credit (Aozora's 底本 edition; Gutenberg's public-domain note). A server behind a mandatory outbound proxy cannot import from sources (uploads still work).

The library lists each book with its author, language, source and number of chapters, and lets you delete it (asked first). If the current library is empty but other workspaces hold books, the page lists them under **Your books in other workspaces** with **Switch to this workspace**.

## Looking words up

Hover a word in the text to see a ranked stack of dictionary entries just below the word (above it only when there is no room below): the word, its reading, chips for its levels (such as "HSK 3", from installed level lists) and frequency, the numbered meanings and the dictionary it comes from. Shift+hover inside a popup opens a popup from inside it. On a touch screen (a phone, a tablet, the Android app) or with a pen, **tap a word** to look it up: the popup stays open until you close it, as for a selection. A drag, a scroll, a long press or a touch that stops the page gliding after a swipe is not a tap; a tap just between two lines finds the nearer one; a long press still selects text, which you need for a highlight or to look up several words at once. Tapping punctuation or a gap closes the open lookup. On a phone the lookup opens as a sheet at the bottom of the screen instead, and the text scrolls so the word stays visible just above the sheet, still marked; close it with its close button, Escape, a drag down or a tap outside. Tapping another word above the sheet closes it and looks that word up in one go; a tap on a gap or a link only closes it. Near the end of a chapter the page gets extra room below, so even the last line can scroll above the sheet. Every entry has an **Add to deck** button that adds the word to the General deck of the book's workspace in one click (a **Choose a deck** menu beside it appears only if the workspace has custom decks). The sentence around the word (up to 200 characters, cut at 。！？ and similar sentence ends) is saved as your own example sentence and linked to the card; an identical sentence is reused. If it cannot be saved the card is added without it and the popup says so. Sentences you save are private to you: other users see only the packs' sentences. Selecting text looks up what starts at the selection ("Showing matches at the start of your selection").

**Inflected Japanese words** are recognised as their dictionary form, and the popup names the inflection (for example `past → completion → te-form`). A verb with a kana helper verb after its te-form is one word: 食べてしまった, 食べちゃった, 勉強しています, 書いておきます, 読んでる, 見てみる, 帰っていく, 教えてくれた, 待ってください are all looked up, coloured, counted and added to decks as the main verb (食べる, 勉強する, 書く, 読む, 見る, 帰る, 教える, 待つ). Requests not to do something, prohibitions and permissions fold the same way: 食べないでください, 行かないでほしい, 泳いではいけない, 食べちゃだめ and 帰ってもいい are 食べる, 行く, 泳ぐ, 食べる and 帰る, and 誤解されかねない is 誤解する. Grammar expressions taught as one unit stay one word: に基づいて, を通して, によれば, に応じて, に沿って, をめぐって, にすぎない, と思ったら (but 計画に基づいている is に + 基づいている, the verb 基づく); a quotation is split (行くと言っていました is と + 言う). A helper written in kanji (走って行く, 持って来る) stays a word of its own, and so does ください on its own (水をください). Common verbs win over rare words written in kanji that share their kana (しました is する, not a rare noun read しま); without a frequency pack some homographs still decide differently (されている is される and して on its own is the noun 仕手, not する), so keep the bundled Jiten list installed. And a particle is not glued to the verb after it (仕事をしている is 仕事 + を + している). A する verb built on a noun shows the noun's reading over the noun only (べんきょう over 勉強 in 勉強しています). When a dictionary lists the whole phrase as its own word (帰ってくる, 書いとく), that entry can come first; a frequency pack (the bundled Jiten list) usually puts the plain verb first.

Words you look up are kept in the workspace's [lookup history](Searching-and-entries#lookup-history) (a popup that stays open a second, one you move into, a tap or a selection); a word you looked up three times or more without a card says so in its popup. Turn recording off on the history page.

## I know this word

Next to **Add to deck** every popup entry has an **I know this** toggle (a check mark shows when it is on). It marks the word as known without making a flashcard: the mark counts like a mastered card everywhere a word is called known (word highlighting, Smart readings, the Words tab, starter decks skip it). Press it again to take the mark back. Marks also come from Settings → Study → **What you already know** (a whole level range or a placement test result), where a declaration can be undone as a whole ([Settings tour](Settings-tour#study)). The mark belongs to the book's workspace and is synced and included in your backup. With word highlighting on, the popup also says how well you know the word ("New word", "Learning", "Known — memory score 85" or "Known — marked by you").

## Display settings

The **Display** tab of the side rail holds the reading display (for Chinese and Japanese books, and books with ruby). The settings are saved per workspace, so they follow you across devices. For Chinese books it also holds the **Script** choice (original, simplified or traditional), kept in this browser.

- **Book ruby** (only for books that have ruby): the reading marks printed in the book.
- **Readings over words**: Never (default), Always or Smart. Chinese words get pinyin; Japanese words with kanji get furigana over the kanji only. A reading is left out when the app cannot align it with certainty; it never guesses. Your own edited readings are used. With pinyin (or bopomofo, or romaji) hidden in Settings → Study → **Romanisation**, no reading of that kind is drawn, the book's own ruby included, and the lookup popup and phone sheet leave it out too; furigana keeps this setting.
- **Smart** hides the reading of a word you know: its flashcard has a memory score at or above a threshold (10 to 100, default 80). A hidden reading is only transparent, so screen readers still read it and the text does not move. Hover a word, tap it (4 seconds), press **R** outside text fields, or use **Show all readings** to reveal them.
- **Ruby size** (Small, Medium, Large, Extra large) and **Font**: **App setting** follows the hanzi and kanji font of Settings → Appearance (its current name in brackets); Sans, Serif or Kai override it for the reader. A face comes from an installed font pack (only the pieces the page's characters need are downloaded) or else your device's fonts.
- **Mark words by how well I know them** (word highlighting, off by default): a **new** word (no flashcard) gets a thin solid underline in the theme's accent, a word you are **learning** (it has a flashcard below the threshold) a thin dotted underline; words you know stay plain. The underline style, not only the colour, tells the two apart (also in Windows high-contrast mode), and the text colour never changes. Nothing is drawn inside your own highlights. The threshold slider shows with Smart or with highlighting on: a word counts as known from that memory score, or when you marked it with **I know this**. Only the first dictionary entry of a word counts, the one whose reading is shown.

These apply in the Reader only; entry pages and flashcards always show readings.

## Words tab and coverage

The **Words** tab of the side rail shows how much of the chapter you can read: "You know 87% of the words in this chapter" with the number large and a thin bar under it, with the counts ("1,234 of 1,420 words · 46 different new words · 12 learning") and how many passages the dictionary does not know (names, rare words). Coverage counts running words: every word with a Chinese character (and, for Japanese, kana), so a word that appears ten times counts ten times; punctuation, digits and Latin letters never count, and passages the dictionary lacks are left out of the percentage. It is rounded down, so 100% means every counted word is known. **Count the whole book** fetches and counts every chapter on demand (with progress and Cancel); the book figure adds up the words of all chapters.

Below is the list of words you do not know yet, most frequent first (50 at a time), each with its reading, how often it appears and whether it is new or learning. Pick a deck and use **Add selected** or **Add all new words** to add them in one go (adding again adds nothing new; a word that already has a card joins the deck), or **I know these** to mark them known (with **Undo**). Word counts are available for Chinese and Japanese books.

## Bookmarks, highlights and notes

At the right edge of the page is a side rail of five icons: **Contents** (the chapters, the one you are reading marked), **Bookmarks**, **Highlights**, **Words** and **Display**. Each icon names itself in a tooltip; the arrow keys, Home and End move between them. Wide screens (1000 px and up) show the open tab's panel between the text and the icons; activating the open tab again collapses the panel. Narrower screens have one button for all five (in the top bar on a phone, above the text on a tablet) that opens them as a sheet at the bottom of the screen; jumping to a chapter, bookmark or highlight, or editing a highlight, closes the sheet first. Above the text a small line names the book and its author and shows a bookmark icon when the paragraph in view is bookmarked; previous and next chapter buttons sit at the end of each chapter. The keys `b`, `h` and `R` work with the panel collapsed or the sheet closed. Whether the panel is open, and which tab, is remembered in this browser.

- **Bookmarks**: **Add bookmark here** or the `b` key bookmarks the paragraph at the top of the screen (again to remove). Its name is the paragraph's beginning (at most 120 characters), and a bookmarked paragraph has a marker in the margin. The tab lists them in book order; you can jump, rename and delete. Up to 200 per book.
- **Highlights**: select text and pick a colour (yellow, green, blue, pink) or **Note** (highlight and add a note) in the popup. The `h` key highlights the current selection in the last colour used. Click a highlight to edit its colour or note, or delete it. On a touch screen a tap on a highlighted word looks the word up instead, and the popup's colours and **Note** act on that highlight (a colour recolours it, **Note** opens its editor, where you can also delete it); a tap on a highlighted character that is no word (punctuation, Latin letters, digits) opens the editor directly; the Highlights tab lists them with **Go to**. A highlight stays inside one paragraph, cannot overlap another, covers at most 500 characters; notes hold up to 2000 characters; a book holds up to 2000 highlights. If the text under a highlight changes (for example after switching between simplified and traditional) it shows a **text changed** badge instead of being drawn in the wrong place.
- Bookmarks, highlights and notes are private and go with their book.

## Reading time

The Reader records active reading time in bursts (a burst under 30 seconds is not recorded, idle gaps count at most 2 minutes, a hidden tab does not count). It feeds the heatmap and the Reading time widget ([Stats and heatmap](Stats-and-heatmap)).

## Not covered by real-device checks

The Reader was verified by unit and component tests; the developers did not test it in real browsers on all devices.
