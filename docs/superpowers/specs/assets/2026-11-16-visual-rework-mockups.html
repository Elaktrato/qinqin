The lotus heatmap

A rough reference for drawing the heatmap stages as a lotus (issue #107): six stages from still water to full bloom, then closed for the night on rest days. They are shown large, at the real heatmap sizes, and in every theme. The heatmap mocks further down use these sketches too. They are placeholders to react to, not final art.

A theme is a named set of colours. On top of it, each person can change the accent, the corner style, the spacing and the font used for hanzi and kanji. The page starts on Catppuccin Latte or Mocha, matching your device's light or dark setting.

Lotus stages

Each stage uses four colour areas the app recolours from the theme: petals in the accent colour, the pad and stem in the theme's green, the seed pod in the warning yellow, and the water in the line colour. Petals that sit further back are the same colour with less opacity, so no outlines are needed.

Notes for drawing

  • Grid: 24 × 24 units; keep the water line near the bottom (y ≈ 19–20) so every stage stands on the same base.
  • Colour areas only: flat fills, no gradients, no outlines, no baked-in colours. Mark each shape as petal, pad/stem, seed pod or water; the app colours them.
  • Depth by opacity: back petals about 70%, middle about 85%, front 100%.
  • Read at 24 px: the bud and the open bloom must be told apart by silhouette alone (tall and narrow vs wide), not by detail.
  • Resting: a closed flower, quieter than the bud (lower opacity), with a small moon. It should not look wilted or sad.
  • Tone: cheerful and colourful. Avoid a lone white lotus on a dark ground (the funerary look).

Reader

The text gets the space; everything else stays out of its way. The reader's side rail becomes a column of icons at the right edge (Contents, Bookmarks, Highlights, Words, Display), shown here with Words open. Hovering or tapping a word opens the lookup popup, a stack of dictionary entries. On the phone the popup becomes a sheet from the bottom, and the rail moves into one button in the top bar.
