# #220 — topic labels for words (2026-11-27)

Milestone v0.9.0, phase 3 (data for courses). Branch `feature/v090-220-topic-labels` from `feature/v0.9.0`.
Issue #220 with the owner decision of 2026-11-26: agent-suggested labels ship once native-language teacher subagents
have reviewed them (several, at higher reasoning), with a confidence and the review notes kept in the file; the owner
reviews later. Design: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-24-courses-design.md` §6 and §9 (labels fill course units by
theme; the course pack and the fill rule are #221).

## What

- A fixed list of **49 topics** shared by all languages (`docs/contracts/topics/topics.json`), stable ids, names in
  `en.json` (`topics.name.<id>`).
- **Labels from pictures:** a word's OpenMoji picture (#216 `map.tsv`) gives the topic of the emoji's group and
  subgroup (`scripts/topics/openmoji-topics.tsv`, written for the project).
- **Agent labels** for HSK 1–4 (bundled `hsk-words`) and ≈ N5–N3 (the #219 estimate), suggested by a labelling agent
  in batches, reviewed by two teacher agents per language independently, disagreements decided by a third.
- **Senses:** a label row may name a sense (1-based, the importer's numbering) where a word's common senses fall
  into different topics.
- **Storage:** `server/internal/topics/labels/<lang>.json` (embedded in the server, CC BY-SA 4.0 since ruling 18, one label per line so
  a change is a one-line diff), read by `server/internal/topics` for #221's fill rule. Contract `topics/`.
- **Coverage report** per level (`scripts/topic-coverage.mjs`), numbers below; thresholds in Go and script tests.

## Rulings

Format: `Ruling: <what> — <why> — <cost if wrong>`.

1. Ruling: **49 topics, ids one lower-case word** (`food`, `function`, `qualities` …), list order = display order;
   a topic is never renamed or reused, only retired — the ids are also message-key segments (`topics.name.<id>`,
   which allows no `-`), and courses store theme ids — a missing theme later is a new id, cheap.
2. Ruling: **Grammar and general words get topics too** (`function`, `qualities`, `actions`, `movement`, `thinking`,
   `communication`, `measures`, `numbers`) — HSK 1–3 is full of words no theme owns (的, 很, 觉得), and the fill rule
   needs every word placed — the course may treat those topics as "spread over all units", which #221 decides.
3. Ruling: **The labels file is embedded in the server** (`server/internal/topics/labels/`), like the Starter sets,
   not a pack — #221 defines the course pack that will carry the labels (design §9); until then the server is the only
   reader and an embedded file needs no install step — if #221 moves the labels into the course pack, the file format
   stays and only its place changes.
4. Ruling: **A label names a word by its dictionary key and by the list's form and reading** (zh key = simplified
   form; ja key = `j:<ent_seq>`, form and reading as the level list gives them), sense 0 or absent = the whole word —
   the fill rule starts from level-list entries (form, reading), which a key alone does not reach without a dictionary
   lookup, and the key keeps the sense number meaningful — a re-estimated jlpt-words list (#219) whose form differs
   from the labelled one misses its label until the agent step runs again for the new words (the coverage report and
   the Go test show it).
5. Ruling: **Lookup = the word row first, then its sense rows; form must match; the reading must match when both
   have one; a kana word with no row of its own matches rows with that reading** — union of topics in that order —
   simple, deterministic, and the same on every client — a homograph of two list entries with different readings
   but no reading in the label shares the label.
6. Ruling: **Review protocol:** a labelling agent suggests; two teacher agents per language (different personas,
   high reasoning) review every word independently (ok or a full correction with a reason); where their versions
   differ a third teacher agent decides, seeing both versions and reasons — independent double review plus an
   adjudicator is how a review panel works, and nobody's single opinion overrides another unseen — the teachers saw the
   suggestion (anchoring); a blind second labelling would catch more but doubles the disagreements.
7. Ruling: **Confidence is the review outcome, not a model guess:** 0.95 both teachers confirmed, 0.90 both made the
   same change, 0.80 adjudicated to one teacher's version, 0.70 the adjudicator's own, 0.50 disputed without a
   decision, 0.60 picture-only (no teacher review) — reproducible from the committed answers — a reader that wants a
   calibrated probability must not read it as one.
8. Ruling: **Words outside the lists keep their picture labels** (basis `picture`, review `picture`, confidence 0.6,
   not teacher-reviewed) — free, deterministic and useful for later levels, the Starter mode and #219's re-estimates —
   about one picture match in ten is doubtful (#216 ruling 8), so these labels are marked and the fill rule may skip
   them by basis.
9. Ruling: **The committed batch files hold the words and the answers, not the dictionary senses**; the prompts get
   the senses from the bundled CC-CEDICT and JMdict at run time — the labels file stays project-written with
   no gloss text, and the batches can be audited answer by answer — re-running the agent step needs the bundled
   dictionaries (they are in the repository).
10. Ruling (superseded by ruling 18): **Licence: the labels files are CC BY 4.0** (project-written: the topic list, the group table, the
    agents' and teachers' choices); they carry words (forms, readings, JMdict sequence numbers) as the Starter sets do,
    no glosses, no emoji ids; `scripts/topics/openmoji-groups.tsv` (concept → group, subgroup, from the pinned
    `openmoji.json`) is OpenMoji/Unicode data and keeps its notice (`scripts/topics/NOTICE.md`, CC BY-SA 4.0) — a topic
    is the project's classification, informed by but not copied from the BY-SA mapping — **flagged for the owner:** if
    the picture-basis labels are judged a BY-SA derivative, relicense the files as CC BY-SA 4.0 (one field and two
    notices).
11. Ruling: **The HSK list is used as a gate** (which words get agent labels), as #216 ruling 9 did; the labels file
    holds no levels — the HSK list has no reuse licence; the labels are keyed by dictionary words — the labels file's
    selection of words still reflects HSK 1–4: **flagged** with #216's open question.
12. Ruling: **Japanese words: the #219 estimate as it stood on its branch on 2026-11-27** (≈ N5 800, ≈ N4 700,
    ≈ N3 2,200 words), passed to the agent step with `--list`; this base has no `jlpt-words` pack — it is the list the
    course will fill from; JMdict common words by frequency would label many words the course never uses — when #219
    lands with a different list, the Go coverage test and `node scripts/topic-coverage.mjs` report the gap; rerun
    `scripts/topic-agents.mjs plan/run ja` for the new words.
13. Ruling: **Senses shown to agents: the listed reading's senses first, at most 6 senses of 4 glosses** — the
    label is about what a learner meets — a rare sense never gets its own row.
14. Ruling: **Contract 21** (base at 20): new folder `topics/` (list read by Go and web, labels file format and lookup
    read by Go), the `topics` key area in the i18n contract — renumbered by the orchestrator on merge.
15. Ruling: **Sources page: one "Built into the app" credit** ("qinqin topic labels", reviewed by agents; CC BY-SA 4.0 since ruling 18),
    not a new section — the data is in the server like the Starter sets and has no pack list to show — when #221 puts
    the labels into the course pack, the pack's own credit replaces it.
16. Ruling: **A sense check after the review:** the adjudicating teacher agent (stage `d`) re-reads every sense row
    the decision kept and keeps only senses common at these levels that a teacher would teach in that unit — the
    Japanese labeller added sense rows for many rare JMdict senses (山 "climax" → arts) that the two reviewers let
    through, and the lookup unions sense rows into the word's topics — a useful rare-sense row may have gone; the
    owner's review can add it back.
17. Ruling: **Chinese labels carry no reading** (a label is the simplified-form entry with all its readings; the
    readings are shown to the agents only) — HSK lists a form with one reading per level (还 hái, 还 huán), and a
    joined "hái / huán" never matched a list entry's reading — two readings of one form share their labels; a sense
    row tells them apart where it matters.
18. Ruling (orchestrator, 2026-11-28, deciding the flag of ruling 10 under the owner's "licensing first" rule): **the
    topic labels files are CC BY-SA 4.0**, not CC BY 4.0 — the labels are derived partly from OpenMoji's groups
    (CC BY-SA 4.0) and are keyed to, and were made from the senses of, CC-CEDICT and JMdict (both CC BY-SA 4.0);
    ShareAlike is the conservative choice and compatible with everything else bundled — the labels are less reusable
    than under CC BY; the owner can loosen this later. Done on `feature/v090-220-topic-licence`: the generator writes
    `"license": "CC-BY-SA-4.0"` and an attribution that credits qinqin contributors, CC-CEDICT or JMdict and OpenMoji;
    the server accepts only that licence; LICENSING.md, `docs/sources.md`, the contract README, the wiki, the Sources
    credit and `scripts/topics/NOTICE.md` say so. No separate LICENSE file: the labels are embedded, not a pack, and
    the NOTICE points at the legal code (the same text as `plugins/data/cedict/LICENSE`). The topic list (ids and
    scopes) stays the project's CC BY 4.0.
