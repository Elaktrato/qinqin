# #228 — look-alike characters (未/末, 己/已/巳, ソ/ン, シ/ツ) (2026-11-28)

Milestone v0.9.0, phase 5 (plan §3 "Drills framework", §2 content review). Branch `feature/v090-228-lookalikes` from
`feature/v0.9.0` (c9793bfd, ContractVersion 37). A drill on the framework (`docs/superpowers/plans/2026-11-28-drills-framework.md`,
`docs/contracts/drills/`), after #180 (tone pairs), #178 (conjugation) and #179 (counters).

## What (issue and owner decisions)

- **Look-alike sets** for hanzi, kanji and kana (未/末, 己/已/巳, 人/入, 土/士, 日/曰, 鸟/乌, 天/夫; ソ/ン, シ/ツ,
  ク/ケ/タ), a hand-written list released CC BY 4.0, reviewed by native-language teacher agents (owner: the owner
  reviews it later). Only sets where at least one character is in the learner's study.
- **The drill:** show one character and pick what it is among its look-alikes (readings and meanings on the answer
  side), or the reverse: from a meaning or a word, pick the right character. Spaced practice **per set** (owner: a
  drill, an item is a set with its own FSRS schedule).
- **Highlights:** where the characters differ (the stroke or component), drawn on the answer.
- **On entry pages:** "Easily confused with: 末" with links.
- **Mistake notebook (#227):** confusions found in writing cards and tests feed this drill — #227 is not built yet; the
  answer events carry what was asked and what was given so it can read them (see Rulings).
- **Shared rules** (item keys, the set format, how answers are checked) in a contract with fixtures, contract **40**
  (37 on the base; #225 takes 38 and #349 39; the orchestrator renumbers).
- **Phone first:** 360 px with no sideways scroll (paths in `phone-width.spec.ts`), hidden romanisation through
  `useRomanisation()`, the Android WebView shell.

## The design

### The sets (`apps/web/src/lib/features/practice/lookAlikes/data/`)

Two files the project writes, `look-alikes-zh.json` and `look-alikes-ja.json` (`"license": "CC-BY-4.0"`, the credit,
a `NOTICE` beside them, `REVIEW.md` with the teachers' notes), bundled in the web app and loaded lazily (a dynamic
import) by the drill:

- `characters`: every character once, with what the answer shows: Chinese `pinyin` and `bopomofo` (the server's own
  converter checks the bopomofo), a short English `meaning` and an `example` word (`word`, its reading, its meaning);
  Japanese kanji the kana `readings` (on in katakana, kun in hiragana), `meaning`, `example`; kana the `kana` script,
  the `romaji` and an example word; and the character's stroke count (checked against the bundled stroke index).
- `sets`: each set lists its `members` (2 to 5 characters, in the set's order), optionally its Chinese `script`
  (`Hans` or `Hant`; absent: both), and per member a `note` (what to look at: "the upper stroke is shorter") and the
  `marks`, the 0-based strokes that make the difference, in the stroke data's order.
- The **item key** is the set's members in order (`未末`, `己已巳`), the planned shape of `drills/README.md`.

### The drill (`look-alikes`, `lib/features/practice/lookAlikes/`)

- **Applies to** Chinese and Japanese. **Candidates:** the sets with a member in the learner's study — a character
  of the written form of any card with a review (word or character card) — in the order of that first review;
  Japanese **kana** sets are always candidates, before the kanji sets; a Chinese set only in its script.
- **Modes:** `recognise` — the character, large; tap its meaning among the set's (each with its reading where the
  romanisation settings show it; a kana's sound is its romaji, or, with romaji hidden, the same kana in the other
  script). `choose` — an example word with the character left out (＿来, "future"), pick the character among the set.
  Which member is asked is random; the mode alternates by the item's answer count (`recognise` first).
- **Grades:** right Good (3), wrong Again (1). The event adds `asked` and `given` (the characters) after the grade.
- **The answer** lists every member: the character drawn from the stroke data with the `marks` in the accent colour
  (the font's glyph when there is no data or the stroke count differs), its readings (romanisation settings), its
  meaning, the note and the example word.

## Rulings

- Ruling: **the sets are written by hand, not derived** from KanjiVG components, Unihan or the radicals — the
  components pack is KanjiVG (CC BY-SA 3.0, accepted only as its own pack, never embedded): a list derived from it
  would be an adaptation under BY-SA 3.0 and could not be released CC BY 4.0 or bundled; Unihan (Unicode licence) has
  no look-alike field except `kSpoofingVariant`, which is about identical glyphs, not learner confusions; a teacher's
  list of classic confusions is what the drill needs — cost if wrong: fewer sets than a derivation would find (more
  sets can be added and reviewed later).
- Ruling: **the bundled data that does help** is used at run time or in tests only: the stroke data (`/hanzi/`, the
  installed strokes packs) draws the highlights; the web's stroke index (`writing/strokeIndex.json`, generated from
  Hanzi Writer data) and the `hanzi-writer-data` dev dependency check each character's stroke count and marks in a
  test; the server's pinyin converter checks the bopomofo — nothing third-party is copied into the sets — cost: none.
- Ruling: **the sets are bundled in the web app** (`lib/features/practice/lookAlikes/data/`, a lazy chunk), not served
  by a route — they are small (a few tens of KB), project-written (CC BY 4.0, like the script charts in the web
  build), need no dictionary, and the drill then works offline and can never make the Practice page fail to load —
  cost if wrong: a correction ships with a web build (as the script charts do); the server never reads them (Go
  checks them in a test).
- Ruling: **item key = the members in order** (`未末`), the drills README's planned shape; a set's members are 2–5
  distinct characters, each one code point; no two sets of a file have the same key, a key is never another's
  members reordered — cost if wrong: changing a set's members starts its schedule afresh (the old key's answers stay
  in the log and count nowhere for the new set).
- Ruling: **the readings and meanings are hand-written in the sets** (pinyin, bopomofo, kana readings, a short English
  meaning, an example word), not read from the installed dictionary — a short teaching meaning ("not yet", "end")
  tells the members apart better than a dictionary's first gloss, and the drill needs no dictionary request — cost if
  wrong: the meanings are English only (as the dictionaries' glosses are) and need their own review.
- Ruling: **"in the learner's study"** is a character of the written form (the card's snapshot) of any card with at
  least one review, word or character card, in the order of that first review — 未来 brings 未末; a card without a
  snapshot (added before snapshots existed) brings nothing — cost if wrong: an unreviewed new card does not bring its
  sets yet.
- Ruling: **kana sets are always candidates** in a Japanese workspace (before the kanji sets) — every learner of
  Japanese reads kana from the first day, and kana are not cards here — cost if wrong: a learner who has not reached
  katakana sees katakana sets (the Practice list shows them as new).
- Ruling: Chinese sets carry an optional **`script`** (`Hans`/`Hant`): a `zh-Hant` workspace takes the `Hant` and
  unmarked sets, other Chinese workspaces the `Hans` and unmarked ones — 鸟乌 is a simplified pair, 鳥烏 its
  traditional one, 未末 both; without it a learner of 本 would get both 木本术 and a traditional duplicate — cost:
  none.
- Ruling: **two modes**, `recognise` (the character → its meaning among the set's) and `choose` (an example word with
  the character left out, ＿来 "future" → the character among the set's); the mode **alternates** with the item's
  number of answers (`recognise` first), the member asked is random — the issue's two directions; a word with a gap
  is the natural "which one is it" and works with every romanisation hidden — cost if wrong: a learner cannot pick the
  direction.
- Ruling: **the prompt of `choose` shows no reading** (the word's reading would name the missing character's
  sound); the answer shows it — cost: none.
- Ruling: **a kana's "meaning"** in `recognise` is its sound: the romaji while romaji show, else the same kana in the
  other script (ソ → そ, ぬ → ヌ) — hidden romaji are never shown (#284), and every learner who reads one script knows
  the other's sound by its pair — cost if wrong: a learner who knows hiragana only meets katakana as the answer to a
  hiragana set.
- Ruling: **grades** right Good (3), wrong Again (1) — the question is a choice among 2–5 with no hint step — cost:
  none.
- Ruling: the answer event adds **`asked` and `given`** after `grade` (`{item, mode, grade, asked, given}`: the
  character asked and the one picked, which is `asked` when right) — the framework allows members after the three, and
  the mistake notebook (#227) needs the confusion, not just the set — cost: two short strings per answer.
- Ruling: **highlights are drawn from the stroke data**: each member's `marks` (0-based stroke indices in stroke
  order) are filled in the accent colour, the other strokes in the text colour, with the member's note in words beside
  it; the font's glyph without marks where the stroke data is missing, fails, or has another stroke count than the
  set's — the stroke data is the shape the writing cards teach; the note is the cue that is not a colour — cost if
  wrong: a stroke pack that orders a character's strokes differently highlights the wrong stroke (the count check
  catches most of it).
- Ruling: **kana have no marks**, only notes — their differences are direction and position (シ's last stroke rises,
  ツ's falls), which a coloured stroke does not show better than words; the kana stroke data (KanjiVG, Japanese only)
  is not in the repository to check marks against — cost: kana answers show the glyphs and the notes.
- Ruling: sets whose members are **the same glyph in ordinary fonts** (へ/ヘ, ー/一) are left out — a drill cannot ask
  what cannot be seen — cost: none (the review extended this to the kana/kanji pairs, below).
- Ruling: **how a question shows the characters** (fonts and themes): in the learner's hanzi face
  (`appearance.hanziFont`, an installed font pack first, then the system stack) for the workspace's script through
  `lang` (zh-Hans, zh-Hant, ja; never left to a fallback font of another region, which changes 今, 令, 骨), at a
  **regular weight** with `font-synthesis: none` (a bold or synthesised bold closes 己's gap and merges 候's short
  stroke), the asked character at `clamp(4rem, 22vw, 6rem)` and the choice glyphs at 3.5 rem (56 px, the teachers'
  floor for 己/已 at phone size), in the theme's `--text` on its background (every theme's contrast is tested); the
  answer draws the members from the **stroke data** (the shapes the writing cards teach, one source for the glyph and
  its highlight) with the marked strokes in `--accent` and the others in the muted text colour `--readable-muted` (lighter: a cue that is not a hue; forced
  colours: Highlight and GrayText), the font's glyph where there is no data or another stroke count — the learner
  practises in the face they read in, and the teachers' "pin a CJK font, large, regular weight" is met without a new
  font — cost if wrong: a learner who picked a decorative face sees the look-alikes in it (the answer's stroke drawing
  still shows the reference shape).
- Ruling: ContractVersion **40** (37 on the base; #225 38, #349 39; the orchestrator renumbers).
- Ruling (made while building): **a drill's own payload members** go through the framework (`DrillAnswerInput.extra`,
  `answerEvent`, `drillPayload`; Go `drills.Payload(…, Member…)`), after item, mode and grade and never replacing them,
  pinned by a `drills.json` build case — the README allowed it but no builder could pass one — cost: none for the
  other drills (no extra).
- Ruling (made while building): **no route, no server code beyond the registry** — the sets are web data; Go checks
  the committed files in a test (keys, duplicates, bopomofo = its converter's) — cost if wrong: an Android client that
  is not the web app reads the files from the repository.
- Ruling (made while building): the choices are in **set order**, not shuffled — the member asked is random, so the
  right position changes, and a fixed order shows the look-alikes side by side the same way each time — cost: none.
- Ruling (made while building): the answer **draws every Han member from the stroke data**, not only the marked ones,
  asked in the set file's language (`?lang=ja` prefers the KanjiVG pack) — one look for the whole list — cost: up to
  five cached `/hanzi/` requests per answer.
- Ruling (made while building, review): **rare members are dropped**, not gated by a per-member flag (adjudicator):
  Chinese 巳, 曰, 侯, 狠; Japanese 已/巳 (with 己's set), 曰, 侯, 烏 — a set joins when any member is studied, so 日 or
  时候 would bring 曰 or 侯 to every beginner; one rule stays simple — cost if wrong: a learner who studies 巳 gets no
  set for it.
- Ruling (made while building, review): the **kana/kanji pairs** カ/力, ロ/口, エ/工, ニ/二, タ/夕, ハ/八 are dropped; オ/才 and
  チ/千 stay — the pairs are one glyph at any size, a single character cannot be judged, and the drill has no "whole
  word" question (a follow-up could add one) — cost: the drill does not train telling kana from kanji in context.
- Ruling (made while building, review): **Japanese marks only where the Chinese and Japanese stroke orders agree** (田
  and 由 have notes but no mark; 甲, 申 are marked) — the app draws the Chinese (Hanzi Writer) data for Japanese where
  the KanjiVG strokes pack is not installed, so a Japanese-order index would light the wrong stroke; a stroke-count
  mismatch (象 12, 像 14 in Japanese, 11 and 13 in the Chinese data) falls back to the font's glyph — cost: 田/由 show no
  highlight.
- Ruling (made while building, review): **no Taiwan readings** (adjudicator) — the app's readings are the mainland
  standard (CC-CEDICT) everywhere; a licence-compatible Taiwan source is a later issue (the MOE dictionary is CC
  BY-ND); new examples avoid words whose reading differs — cost: a zh-Hant learner sees 眼睛 yǎn jing.
- Ruling (made while building): **entry pages** name the look-alikes of the **Han characters** of the written form, in
  the workspace's script when it reads the entry's language, each a link to its search (`/search?q=末`); kana
  look-alikes stay in the drill (they would show on every kana word's page); shown whatever the Practice switch — it is
  information about the character, not practice — cost: a word page of 未来 also says 未 → 末.
- Ruling (made while building): the Practice page's own DOM test uses a language no registered drill takes (`es`) —
  Japanese now always lists the look-alike drill (its kana sets), and the test is about the sample drill — cost: none.
- Ruling (made while building): the **mistake notebook (#227)** is not built yet; the answer events carry `asked` and
  `given`, so it can read the confusions later; feeding writing-card confusions into this drill belongs to #227 —
  cost: none now.
