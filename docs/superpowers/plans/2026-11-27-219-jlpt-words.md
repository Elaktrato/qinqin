## What

A deterministic generator `scripts/prepare-jlpt-words.mjs` and the bundled `levels` pack
`plugins/data/jlpt-words` (scheme "JLPT words (approximate)", levels "≈ N5" … "≈ N1", unit `word`), built only from
bundled data: the Jiten frequency ranks (`jiten-frequency`, CC BY-SA 4.0), JMdict (`jmdict`, CC BY-SA 4.0, EDRDG) and
the JLPT kanji estimate (`jlpt-kanji`, MIT + CC BY-SA 4.0). The pack is picked up by the generic levels machinery:
badges, the map's grouping, level sessions, level decks, the Sources page. No contract or pack type change.

## The estimate

- **Words:** one Jiten rank is one word (Jiten lists every written form and reading of a word with the same rank).
  Each rank's rows are matched to one JMdict entry by written form **and** reading (a kana row by its kana); the
  entry matching most rows wins, ties broken by usually-kana/kana-only entries first, then JMdict priority, then
  sequence number. A rank no entry matches is reported in `UNMATCHED.tsv`, never guessed.
- **kanjiLevel:** the hardest `jlpt-kanji` level among the Han characters of the entry's written form (々 ignored);
  a kanji without a JLPT level counts as ≈ N1. A kana-only or usually-kana (first sense `uk`) word has none.
- **rankLevel:** Jiten rank ≤ R5 → ≈ N5, ≤ R4 → ≈ N4, ≤ R3 → ≈ N3, ≤ R2 → ≈ N2, ≤ R1 → ≈ N1, beyond R1 → not listed.
- **Estimate:** the harder of the two.
- **Thresholds:** fitted so the cumulative size at each level matches the commonly cited sizes (≈ 800, 1,500,
  3,700, 6,000, 10,000); with the "harder of two" rule each cumulative count depends on one threshold only, so the
  fit is exact and deterministic. Named constants with the numbers in a comment.
- **Optional base list (`--base <dir>`):** n5.csv … n1.csv with `expression` and `reading` columns (the format of
  the Waller-derived open-anki-jlpt-decks files, parsed with the existing `vocabForms`). Base words are matched to
  JMdict by form and reading (unmatched and ambiguous reported per level); matched words keep their base level, the
  rest get the estimate; `--calibrate --base` fits the thresholds on 80 % of the base words and reports per-level
  agreement on the 20 % held out. Only after the licence is confirmed (local follow-up).

## Rulings

- Ruling: the base list is an **optional input that is not used** — owner decision 5 (2026-11-26, `docs/v090-plans`)
  says no Waller list, the v0.9.0 plan §6 asks for it as an optional input with the licence check as a local step;
  both hold if the code reads it but the shipped pack does not use it — cost if wrong: none for the shipped data; the
  `--base` path would be dead code (about 120 lines with tests).
- Ruling: **one Jiten rank is one word**, matched to the JMdict entry that has most of its rows (form + reading, kana
  rows as readings); ties go to kana-written entries, then more priority tags, then the lower sequence number — Jiten
  lists all forms and readings of one word under one rank (これ: 此れ, 是, 之 … all rank 34) — cost if wrong: a tie
  picks a homograph (11,636 ties overall, mostly particles and beyond the cut).
- Ruling: **usually-kana and search-only-kanji words have no kanji level** (first sense `uk`, or every kanji form
  `sK`) — する is written 為る in JMdict and 為 is N1; the particle の is searchable as 乃 — cost if wrong: a few kana
  words one level too easy.
- Ruling: a kanji with **no JLPT level counts as N1** (the plan's "beyond") — cost if wrong: a word with a rare kanji
  shows ≈ N1 when it could be easier.
- Ruling: the **cut at ≈ N1 is 10,000 words cumulative** — the plan says "the rest at N1" without a size; 10,000 is
  the commonly cited N1 vocabulary size, and the 590,000 Jiten rows cannot all be N1 — cost if wrong: N1 is too
  short or too long; one constant.
- Ruling: **grammar expressions are left out**: JMdict entries that are only `exp` and whose reading starts with a
  particle or the copula (の に と で か が は も を へ だ な や): のだ, として, かもしれない, だろう (160 within the cut) —
  the JLPT tests these as grammar; set phrases (ありがとう, お願いします, いただきます) are `exp` too but start
  otherwise and stay — cost if wrong: a few set phrases that start with one of those kana are left out too.
- Ruling: levels are named **"≈ N5" … "≈ N1"** and the scheme "JLPT words (approximate)" — the issue asks for "≈" in
  the scheme name, notice and badges; the badge reads "≈ N5 · JLPT words (approx.)" — cost if wrong: one regenerate.
- Ruling: the pack is **CC BY-SA 4.0** (`LICENSE` = the CC BY-SA 4.0 legal code from the jmdict pack) and carries
  kanjiapi.dev's MIT text as `LICENSE-kanjiapi.dev` — an adaptation of the Jiten ranks and JMdict (per the plan's
  Ruling 1); the kanji levels feed the computation and their notice is kept — cost if wrong: a wording fix.
- Ruling: a kana-only word is written as its **kana string**, a kanji word as `{form, reading}` with its first

### Root cause: the kanji estimate's design, not a bug

The kanji levels of 暑 嫌 僕 傘 誕 丈 慢 慮 惑 (N1) and 練 机 卵 菜 (N2) come from the **base list** kanjiapi.dev `jlpt.tsv`,
which keeps the pre-2010 four-level scale: these kanji are KANJIDIC2 old level 1 (old level 2 for 練 机 卵; 菜 old 3 →
kanjiapi N2; 暑 is old level 3, kanjiapi still says N1, the one odd row). 椅 鍵 誰 箸 have no old level; they are
grade-8 jōyō kanji, and rule b of the gap fill (`2026-11-14-jlpt-approx.md`) puts grade 8 at N1 as designed. So the
kanji generator does what its plan says; the JLPT kanji levels are about **writing** a kanji, and a word is often
learnt spoken or in kana long before its kanji. jlpt-kanji is left unchanged.

- Ruling: **the kanji estimate stays; the word rules limit its effect** — a kanji level answers "when do learners
  write it", not "when do they know the word" — cost if wrong: kanji levels at N1 for 暑, 嫌 still show on the kanji
  badges (a separate question for jlpt-kanji; not in this issue).

### New rules (`levelFor` in `scripts/prepare-jlpt-words.mjs`)

1. **Core list** (`scripts/jlpt-words/core.tsv`, 538 N5/N4 textbook words, CC BY 4.0, project-written, reviewed by
   agents): sets its words' level directly, always (ranked or not).
2. **Left out**: the curated `grammar.tsv` (たら, じゃない, なんです, のだ, ようになる …); `exp`-only grammar expressions
   that start with a particle or the copula **and have no kanji-first form** (so 初めまして, 申し訳ありません, 何も, 構わない
   stay); `exp`-only entries without a common-word tag (ichi1, spec1, spec2, news1: 死んでいる, もう無理, どうしたん);
   set phrases word + を/が/に + verb (言葉を失う); written numbers and numeral + counter (５分, 十人, 一台, 一ヶ月); kana
   particles (な, ね: the JLPT tests them as grammar); noise (repeated letters あああ; clipped sounds っつ, ゲッ, あっ);
   one-kana fragments that are not particles (げ).
3. **Frequency first**: the Jiten rank level (cuts fitted on the rank alone, see below).
4. **Kanji raise at most one level**, and **not at all for a word with any JMdict priority tag**; kana, usually-kana,
   ateji (綺麗) and non-jōyō spellings count as kana words (no kanji effect).
5. **Priority lift** (the review's third signal, reshaped, see the ruling): a word the rank puts at ≈ N2 or harder goes
   two levels easier if it is on JMdict's basic list (ichi1) within nf01–nf06, one level if ichi1 within nf07–nf12 or
   tagged spec1 or gai1; never easier than ≈ N3. ichi1 + nf01–nf12 words are in even beyond the last cut.
6. **Outside the core, ≈ N4 or harder**, except kana pronouns, conjunctions and auxiliaries (これ, でも).
7. **Register floor**: a word whose first sense is slang, vulgar or colloquial, or an interjection not on JMdict's
   basic lists, is ≈ N3 or harder.

### Rulings (fix wave)

- Ruling: **the review's hard caps became a lift** — measured on the bundled data, "nf01–nf06 → ≤ N4" caps about
  1,600 of the list's words and "news1/ichi1/spec1/nf01–nf12 → ≤ N3" about 6,300, against the commonly cited 1,500
  and 3,700 words up to those levels; they filled ≈ N4/N3 with newspaper terms (首相, 知事, 民間, 癒着) and collapsed the
  rank cuts (R5 = R4 = R3 = 739, so 風呂 fell to ≈ N2). Tighter caps (ichi1 + nf01–nf03 → ≤ N4) still collapsed them.
  The lift keeps the intent (common daily-life words are easier than media frequency says) — cost if wrong: some
  newspaper-common words sit one level too easy (bounded at ≈ N3).
- Ruling: **the lift stops at ≈ N3, and outside the core nothing is ≈ N5 except function words** — the first blind
  teacher spot check (below) put the non-core words the rank made ≈ N5 (先輩, 平気, 程度, 更に, 意外, 母親) mostly at N3
  and the lifted newspaper words (急性, 麻薬, 名目) at N1; ≈ N5 is the textbook core — cost if wrong: frequent N5
  words missing from the core show at ≈ N4 (one level off, and the core list can be extended).
